Where Tennessee probate is actually filed

Every county, the office that takes the filing, and the number that reaches it. Eighty-four counties use the Clerk and Master. Eleven do not, and walking into the wrong office costs you a morning.

Last verified October 27, 2026 County filing offices checked against each county's own published pages. Rutherford's phone corrected back to the county's published number after an unverifiable one was found in place.

Why this page exists

No published list of this exists. Tennessee's default rule sends probate to Chancery Court and the Clerk and Master, but individual counties have private acts that move it, and a 2003 statute barring county clerks from serving probate courts carved out exemptions by population bracket. The result is a patchwork that statewide guides flatten and get wrong. This was built by reading the two controlling statutes and every county's currently effective private acts.

The nine exceptions

If the estate is in one of these counties, the Clerk and Master is the wrong office.

  • Davidson. Seventh Circuit Court, Probate Division. File with the Circuit Court Clerk. The Clerk and Master here does not take probate.
  • Shelby. A standing Probate Court with its own separately elected clerk, in Room 124.
  • Putnam. Probate Court under a General Sessions judge, clerked by the Circuit Court Clerk.
  • Loudon. One officeholder is the Circuit, Criminal, Sessions, Juvenile and Probate Court Clerk. File there, at the Justice Center in Lenoir City.
  • Rutherford. General Sessions judges sit as Probate Judge. File with the County Clerk, in Murfreesboro, not at one of its other three offices.
  • Jefferson. County Clerk, which runs its own probate section with its own intake form and fee schedule.
  • Rhea. County Clerk. A 1982 act vested probate exclusively in General Sessions and expressly divested the Clerk and Master.
  • Sevier. County Clerk's probate clerks. Ask for option 5.
  • Cannon. County Clerk, under a population exemption at T.C.A. 18-6-106.

Two more, Scott and Lauderdale, are unsettled in state law. The reasons are under their entries below.

Nine further counties have a probate court sitting outside chancery while the Clerk and Master still takes the filing: Wilson, Cumberland, Dyer, Monroe, Roane, White, Henry, Hamilton, and Knox. Right office, unusual court.

Why there are no addresses here

Because the official ones disagree. Checking a single county turned up three different addresses for the same office across three state and county sources, all of them current-looking. Another county's office had moved to a new courthouse while the state directory still listed the old building.

Phone numbers held up where addresses did not. So this page gives you the number, and the person who answers it will tell you which door, what it costs today, and whether you need an appointment. That is a better answer than a street address that was right last year.
