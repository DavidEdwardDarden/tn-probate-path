Probate in Rutherford County, Tennessee

Which office takes the filing in Murfreesboro, what it costs, the local rules that will stop you at the door, and which of the three Tennessee paths your estate is on.

Rutherford is not a Clerk and Master county

Most Tennessee counties file probate with the Clerk and Master of the Chancery Court. Rutherford does not. By private act the General Sessions judges sit as Probate Judge, and every probate document is filed with the County Clerk. Sending someone to the Clerk and Master here costs them a trip, and most statewide guides get this wrong.

Where it is filed

The County Clerk's office receives the petition, issues the letters, takes the claims, and holds the file until the estate closes. The General Sessions judge hears the matter and makes the determinations.

Before you go

Local rules that change what you should do, and that a statewide guide will not tell you.

  • There is an enforced dress code, and you may appear before the judge. No tank tops, no shorts, no spaghetti straps, no t-shirts with inappropriate writing, no flip-flops. Hats and sunglasses come off.
  • Checks must be drawn on a Tennessee bank. The office refuses out-of-state checks outright. If you are settling a parent's estate from another state, this is the rule that strands you at the window, so bring cash or a card. A card adds 2.25% plus $0.25.
  • Two different payment rules in the same transaction. Court costs take cash, check, or card. The notice to creditors is payable by check only, and it still has to be a Tennessee check.
  • Claims against the estate are filed in triplicate, notarized, with supporting evidence plus two more copies, and a $50.00 check or money order made out to the Rutherford County Clerk. Claim forms come from the clerk.
  • A TennCare release is now required to close. A personal representative's statement about the TennCare account used to be accepted and no longer is. The closing order will not be signed without the actual release.
  • The inheritance tax closing letter from the Department of Revenue also has to be filed before the judge will sign the closing order.
  • Clerks cannot tell you which form to file or how to fill it out. That is legal advice and they are barred from giving it.

The three paths

T.C.A. § 30-4-103

Small estate

Personal property of $50,000 or less with no real estate involved, and 45 days since the death, though the court may waive that wait for good cause. Rutherford charges $229.50. The county's own materials note that a small estate may be heard by the Court or by the County Court Clerk, provided the paperwork is in order and there is no question of law.

Bond is required by statute, as a corporate surety bond equal to the value of the property, whatever the will says. It is excused only if you are the sole heir, or together the only heirs, of someone who died without a will, the sole beneficiary, or together the only beneficiaries, under the will, or every adult heir and beneficiary consents in writing. Bring those consent forms signed by the spouse and all next of kin.

No creditor notice is published and no creditor can file a claim in a small estate. The claim fee and the newspaper costs below belong to the full administration track.

T.C.A. § 30-1-117

Full administration

More than $50,000 in personal property, or real estate to transfer. $418.50 to open and close, including one letter. Notice to creditors is published and claims run four months from first publication.

T.C.A. § 32-2-111

Muniment of title

A will admitted for the limited purpose of establishing title to real estate and personal property, with no administration opened. $418.50 here, the same as a full estate.

What to carry in

  • A certified copy of the death certificate. Order ten; every bank and insurer keeps one.
  • The original will, if there is one. Not a copy.
  • A list of every outstanding debt the person owed. The county asks for this by name on a small estate.
  • Affidavits from any heirs who are not joining in the small estate filing.
  • Consent forms from the spouse and next of kin if bond is to be waived.
  • An asset list with date-of-death values, split into what was in their name alone and what was not. That split decides your path.
  • A checkbook, for the creditor notice, and your photo identification.
