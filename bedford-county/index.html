The rule changed in 2023

Public Chapter 297, effective April 28, 2023, replaced Tennessee's small estate affidavit with a sworn petition for Limited Letters, prescribed a statutory form for the letters, rewrote the bond rules, and barred creditors from filing claims in a small estate. Forms and guides still using the old affidavit wording are out of date. Some county fee schedules still print the old name over the new form.

Where it is filed

Bedford County runs Chancery and Probate as one office. The county calls it the Chancery and Probate Courts, part of the Seventeenth Judicial District, which also covers Lincoln, Marshall, and Moore. Chancellor J. B. Cox presides.

The filing address is 108 Northcreek Drive, Suite 155. That is the address on the Clerk and Master's own office-locations listing. A Public Square address appears on one of the county's scheduling pages and is not where the office sits.

Before you go

Local rules that change what you should do, and that a statewide guide will not tell you.

  • Call to schedule a probate proceeding. The office sets the date; you do not simply appear.
  • A common form petition must come in with the county's Probate Information Sheet, the will if there is one, and any affidavits, waivers, or proposed exhibits.
  • The surety bond amount is set by the Clerk and Master and must be on file before letters issue. Budget for it.
  • A small estate filing not prepared by an attorney must use the county's own Small Estate form. A downloaded generic form will not do.
  • Motion days are usually Fridays but change often, so confirm rather than assume. Contested matters are scheduled through the Chancellor's assistant at 931-438-1956.
  • Notice to creditors is published in the Shelbyville Times Gazette, and that cost is added to the filing fee.

The three paths

T.C.A. § 30-4-103

Small estate, Limited Letters

Personal property in the person's name alone totalling $50,000 or less, counted without any real estate, and 45 days since the death, though the court may waive that wait for good cause. It is available only if nobody has filed to appoint a personal representative in the meantime. You file a sworn petition listing every asset and every debt, and the clerk issues Limited Letters.

Bond is required, not merely possible. The statute says the petitioner shall make a corporate surety bond equal to the value of the property, whatever the will says about waiving it. Three things excuse it: you are the sole heir, or together the only heirs, of someone who died without a will, you are the sole beneficiary, or together the only beneficiaries, under the will, or every adult heir and beneficiary consents in writing.

No creditor notice is published and no creditor may file a claim in a small estate. If an office quotes you a publication cost on this track, ask why.

T.C.A. § 30-1-117

Full administration, common form

More than $50,000 in personal property, or anything the smaller track cannot carry. A personal representative is appointed, notice to creditors is published, and claims run four months from first publication. Most uncontested estates settle in six to twelve months.

T.C.A. § 32-2-111

Muniment of title

A will admitted for the limited purpose of establishing title, with no personal representative appointed and no administration opened. It fits where nothing needs an administrator. Whether it suits a given case is a question for the clerk's office.

What to carry in

  • The original will, if there is one. Not a copy. Tennessee wants the original filed with the clerk, and Letters will not issue until it is in.
  • Certified death certificates. Order ten. Every bank, insurer, and plan administrator keeps one.
  • An asset list with date-of-death values, split into what was in the person's name alone and what was not. That split is what decides your path.
  • A debt list with each creditor's name, address, and amount. The small estate petition asks for this by name.
  • Written consents from the other adult heirs, where they agree you should serve.
  • Your own photo identification.

What the clerk will and will not do

Clerks commonly help with the small estate petition as a matter of local practice, and most will tell you which form, which blanks, where to sign, and what it costs. No statute requires it. The older law gave clerks legal protection for help given in good faith, and the 2023 rewrite removed even that, so treat assistance as a courtesy rather than a right.

They will not tell you whether to open a small estate or a full administration, who inherits, what to do about a disputed will, or how to handle real estate. Those are legal questions, and a clerk answering them would be practicing law. That line is where people get stuck, and it is the line this site exists to help you stand on the right side of.
